01 · The Problem
Why do we need a better TB vaccine?
TB kills 1.3 million people per year. The existing vaccine (BCG) is only 50% effective in adults. MDR-TB is making antibiotics useless. We need a smarter, targeted vaccine.
🦠
What is an Epitope?
When bacteria infect you, your immune system breaks them into tiny fragments called epitopes — like puzzle pieces your immune cells learn to recognise. A vaccine shows these fragments in advance so the immune system is prepared when the real infection arrives.
Length: 8–25 amino acids · Source: TB proteins
🔑
What is HLA?
HLA molecules are like "display stands" on your cells. They grab epitope fragments and hold them up for T-cells to see. Different people have different HLA types — a good vaccine needs epitopes that work across all HLA types worldwide.
44,398 HLA alleles · 9/9 supertypes covered
⚔️
What is a T-Cell?
T-cells are your immune soldiers. CD8+ (killer) T-cells recognise Class I epitopes and destroy infected cells. CD4+ (helper) T-cells recognise Class II epitopes and coordinate the whole immune response. Our vaccine activates both types.
5 Class I epitopes + 8 Class II epitopes selected
💊
Why MDR-TB is different
MDR-TB mutates the proteins that antibiotics target. Antibiotics stop working — but T-cells can still attack the same epitopes. We prioritised epitopes from essential genes that MDR-TB cannot mutate away without killing itself.
inhA epitope ranked #1 · TCR gold standard confirmed

There are 23,884 possible TB epitopes. Which 13 do you put in a vaccine?

The GNN learns to propagate information along edges — an epitope connected to a TCR-confirmed gold standard inherits some of that confidence through the graph structure. This is why the model can predict immunogenicity for epitopes that have never been tested in a lab.
Why a graph? Why not just a sequence model?
A sequence model sees RLPAKAPLL and predicts from amino acids alone. But immunogenicity depends on which protein it came from (essential gene?), which HLA types can present it (population coverage?), and whether a T-cell has ever recognised it (TCR evidence). The graph encodes all of these simultaneously — that is the core novelty.
