What is happening: The amino acid sequence RLPAKAPLL is fed into ESM-2 one token at a time. Each amino acid attends to every other amino acid using transformer attention. The final output is a single 320-dimensional vector — one number per dimension — that encodes the biochemical meaning of the whole sequence. Think of it as compressing a sentence into a fingerprint.
What is happening: Every biological entity becomes a node carrying its embedding vector. The epitope node starts with a 820-dim vector (320 ESM-2 + 500 position-AA features). The protein node has 324 dims. HLA and TCR nodes have their own vectors. At this point nothing is connected — these are just isolated number vectors sitting in space.
What is happening: Each node sends its vector as a "message" to all connected neighbours. The epitope receives messages from: its source protein, its HLA alleles, its TCR (if any), and similar epitopes. These messages arrive simultaneously in round 1. Press the button above to watch the signals flow.
What is happening: Not all messages are equally important. The HANConv attention mechanism assigns a weight between 0 and 1 to each incoming message. A TCR recognition edge gets high weight (strong experimental evidence). A similarity edge to a low-scoring epitope gets low weight. These attention weights are what the GNN learns during training — it learns which connections to trust most.
What is happening: After 4 rounds of message passing, the epitope node holds a 256-dimensional representation that encodes everything known about it from its biological neighbourhood. A 3-layer classifier (256→128→32→1) converts this into a single probability between 0 and 1. Backpropagation compares this against the known label and adjusts all attention weights to reduce the error.
